# sim_robot_core

What a simulation engine node knows about the robots it stands, whatever its physics. The MuJoCo and Isaac Sim nodes of `nodes-hub` both install it in their base image and keep only what is their own: their gains, their model files, their physics and their transport.

| Module | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `registry` | The robots in the scene, by the name each stands under: who may join, who holds which robot, and the pair grace time of each robot: how long it stays without the pairs its model asks for, running from the first time a limb or its base reaches it. A refused join raises `ValueError`, and `StillJoining` when the caller attaches its own robot again while the robot still joins: that one refusal ends once the robot stands, so an engine tells it apart. |
| `pairs` | The six pairing slots an engine declares (`arms`, `grippers`, `bases`, `rgb_cameras`, `rgbd_cameras`, `lidars`) and what each pair names: its robot, and its limb, its base, its camera or its lidar. |
| `models` | One entry per robot model, with its checks, and the table that pairs those entries with an engine's own. |
| `setpoints` | `Setpoints`, what an engine takes on its three driven slots: the latest setpoint of each arm, gripper and base, from the pair of a part its robot's model has, a setpoint the parse refuses dropped and logged once until one of that part's fits. The setpoint of a `joint_link` pair, `JointSetpoint`: one position per joint and no velocities or one per joint, each a number from -1e10 to 1e10 (`MAX_VALUE`, MuJoCo's `mjMAXVAL`), so a setpoint past it is dropped as one that is not a number is, and a base's, `JointSetpoint.parse_wheels`, with one velocity per wheel, which joint_link's leader of a velocity-mode follower always sends. The setpoint of a `gripper_link` pair, `GripperSetpoint`: an opening and a max effort of at least 0, each within the same bound. A base's setpoint belongs to the pair it came on and drives the wheels until more than `LEADER_STREAMING_WINDOW_S`, joint_link's 0.5 s, pass with no setpoint. |
| `object_state` | `RobotRecord`, a robot's record on object_state: the name it stands under, its model, and the world pose of its robot frame with its velocities there in world axes; `RobotRecord.fixed` gives a robot whose robot frame is fixed to the world, at rest. `ObjectRecord`, a spawned object's record, and `ObjectStateSnapshot`, every object's and every standing robot's record at one capture with its instant. |
| `rate_loop` | The rule a MuJoCo model of a base's wheel is held to, `wheel_rate_loop`: one `<velocity>` actuator, its `kv` the loop's proportional gain, and one `<intvelocity>` actuator, its `kp` the loop's integral gain, on the wheel's joint, both of gear 1, the second's activation unclamped. It reads the model's actuator arrays, so the MuJoCo node and the Isaac node's stage builder hold the same MJCF to one rule. |
| `cameras` | The camera configuration of a model's entry, each camera with its frame, where a rendered camera's pixels point as camera_geometry carries it (its pinhole model, and the pose of its depth in its colour), the z16 depth wire format, the capture index, and per-camera frame pacing. |
| `lidars` | The lidar configuration of a model's entry: each 2-D lidar of the description with its frame, its pose in its link and the ranges it measures, swept as the entry gives it (`LidarSweep`: `min_angle_rad`, `angle_increment_rad`, `rays`, `scan_rate_hz`, its fan at most one turn), when each ray of a scan is due and where it points, and a ray's range in lidar_2d's two marks (`UNMEASURED_MARK_M` 0 and `NO_RETURN_MARK_M` 10000). The lidar turns once a scan counterclockwise about its frame's +Z, ray `i` due `round(i x time_increment_s x 1e9)` ns after its scan starts, as Waldo sweeps it. `LidarSweeper` sweeps one lidar of one robot: after each step of the engine, given the step's engine time, where the lidar's frame stands in world and how the engine casts a ray, it casts every ray due since the step before from the frame's pose at the ray's own time, linear in position and spherical in orientation between the two steps, and gives each `Scan` the step completes: when its ray 0 was due, its capture index and its ranges in the marks. A scan starts only while the sweeper's `viewed` says a pair views the lidar. A step that does not move the engine's clock forward drops the scan in progress, and so does `interrupt`, which an engine calls at each rebuild or edit of its scene; the capture index counts on across both. |
| `world` | `world`, the one frame of a simulation: a command with no frame is in world, one in another frame raises `UnknownFrame`, which an engine refuses with `unknown_frame`, and a robot base's target turns about +z alone. A value no contract takes raises control_core_py's `GeometryError`, refused with `rejected`; `failure_kind_of` gives the failure kind of each refusal. `attach_placement` reads where an attach of simulation_robot places its robot: a pose in world, or none for a spot of the engine's choosing, and `rolled_placement` where a robot that rolls stands: where its wheels carried its robot frame, facing the heading they turned it to. |

## The six slots

An engine declares one slot per kind of pair, each holding any number of pairs. Nothing in a slot's name says which robot, limb, base, camera or lidar a pair is, so every pair is read for it:

- a pair's robot is the copy it carries, which is the name the robot attached under. A pair with no copy belongs to a robot launched outside one, and outside a copy there is one robot in the scene for it to belong to;
- a limb pair's limb is the link the pair comes from on the robot's side, so a backbone names its downstream links after its limbs: `left_arm: "simulation_inst/arms"` for an OpenArm, `arm: "simulation_inst/arms"` for an SO-101; a base pair's base is the link its base node pairs it from, `base: "simulation_inst/bases"`, the one name a robot answers to for its base;
- a camera pair's camera and a lidar pair's lidar is its relay's name in the copy (`wrist_left`, `wrist`, `lidar`), which is the relay's instance id without the copy's prefix.

A new robot, limb, base, camera or lidar changes no engine's interface.

A base pair carries the setpoints of the base's wheels, one position per wheel, left then right, with their velocities: an engine runs each wheel at its velocity, as the base's driver runs it in velocity mode, a setpoint that carries no velocities is dropped as one the parse refuses, and the positions, checked as an arm's are, go unused. Its measured state goes back on the same pair. The setpoint belongs to its pair: once a reading of the pairs finds that pair gone, it releases the wheels, which an engine holds where they stand, the integral of each wheel's rate loop cleared, at rate 0 (`Setpoints.keep_paired_bases`, `Setpoints.take_base_release`), and a pair that comes back drives nothing until its leader sends. The wheels follow as `joint_link`'s follower of a velocity-mode device follows: from each setpoint that arrives until more than `LEADER_STREAMING_WINDOW_S`, its 0.5 s, pass with no setpoint, on the simulation clock, the engine's own, and then they hold at rate 0, released the same way (`Setpoints.base`): a base whose leader stops sending holds, whatever its pair does. The engine's clock moves at its steps, so the first step that reads a setpoint starts its window, within a step of its arrival on that clock. An arm holds its last setpoint, the positions it holds still at.

## Model entries

A robot's entry comes from its description, the URDF and SRDF its attach goal carries, read by [`robot_description_py`](../robot_description_py): each arm group under the name the robot answers to in `limb_motion` and `limb_state` with the joints it moves, each gripper group with where its `closed` and `open` states put each finger, its base, under the name `base`, with the wheel joints of the differential base the description gives, left then right, the optical frame of each camera the robot carries with the depths a depth camera measures, the frame of each 2-D lidar with the ranges it measures, and the posture the robot starts in, every arm at its `ready` state and every gripper closed. `src/sim_robot_core/models/<model>.json5` holds what the description does not: how the engines render each camera of the model, by the camera's name, its field of view and its streams, the size of its depth image on a camera that measures depth, under `cameras`, and how they sweep each lidar, by the lidar's name, under `lidars`. The package ships one for `openarm_v2`, one for `so101` and one for `botshare_base`; a model whose description carries no camera and no lidar, such as `openarm_v1`, needs none. An entry that renders a camera the description lacks, or that leaves one of the description's cameras out, is refused at the attach, naming the cameras of both, and a camera of a model this package ships no file for is refused naming the file to create. A camera rendered with a depth stream is one whose description gives it a depth range, the depth sensor of its URDF, and one rendered without is one whose description gives it none; any other is refused at the attach, naming the camera and what to write. The lidars are held the same way: an entry that sweeps a lidar the description lacks, or leaves one of its lidars out, is refused at the attach naming the lidars of both, and a lidar the description gives a range to 10000 m or past it, lidar_2d's mark of no return, is refused naming it.

An engine keeps one entry of its own per model, `<model>.json5` in a directory beside its code, with what it alone knows: the file it loads, its gains, its extras. `Models.read(directory)` reads the engine's entries and the entries this package ships for the same models once, as the engine starts, so an entry that does not read, a key no entry has or a bad render stops the engine before a robot attaches. An engine stands the models it has an entry for: a model with no entry is refused naming the ones the engine stands, and none falls back to another's layout. A robot with a base rolls (`ModelEntry.rolls`): it stands free on the floor, its placement sets where it starts and the heading it starts at, and its wheels carry it from there; a robot without one stands fixed where it is placed. A description that names no arm and gives no differential base is refused, and so is one with arms whose wheels make no differential base, a group of its SRDF holding them among them, each naming the kind of the refusal and why; a description with arms whose base body carries no continuous joint but the ones its arms move stands without a base. Every refusal of an entry or a model, at the engine's start or at an attach, raises `ModelEntryError` (`errors`), a `ValueError` that names the entry or the model and why.

```python
from robot_description_py import RobotDescription
from sim_robot_core.models import Models

models = Models.read(engine_dir / "models")
known = models.of("so101")      # raises, naming the models this engine stands
entry = known.entry(RobotDescription.parse(goal.urdf, goal.srdf))
entry.arm_names()                # ["arm"]
known.engine                     # the engine's own entry, as written
```

A camera's frame is its optical frame, the link `<camera>_optical_frame` of the description, which sensor_mounts gives for the camera's name and each of its pictures names (`CameraConfig.frame`). Its pose is the optical frame, where the fixed joint that holds it places it in its link, turned half a turn about its own X (`cameras.camera_pose`): the optical frame looks along +Z with +Y down the picture, and the engines' camera frame looks along -Z with +Y up it.

## The match

`ModelEntry.mismatch(held)` compares what a robot holds on the six slots with what its model has, and names both lists when they differ: a limb, a base, a camera or a lidar the model lacks, or a limb or the base the model has and no pair drives. A camera or a lidar the model has may go unpaired, since a camera nobody views is not rendered and a lidar nobody views is not swept. An engine starts a robot's pair grace time again only while its pairs match, so a robot paired as another model does not stay; a robot no limb or base reached yet has no pair grace time running (`Registry.note_driven_part_reached` starts it, `Robot.pair_grace_ran_out` reads it), its stay is its attach goal's until one does, and `ModelEntry.holds_every_driven_part(held)`, a pair for every limb and the base, is what a robot's readiness asks.

## Adding a robot

A robot stands in a simulation once its model's description is in [`robot_description`](../robot_description) and each engine has a model of it. Its id is the model's id in `robot_description`, which names every file below:

1. When its description carries a camera or a lidar, write `src/sim_robot_core/models/<id>.json5` with how each camera renders, under the camera's name in the description, with a depth stream on each camera its URDF gives a depth sensor, and how each lidar sweeps, under the lidar's name. A robot with no camera and no lidar needs no file here.
2. Give the MuJoCo node of `nodes-hub` its model: a robot in Waldo's catalogue, packed and published as a release whose `robots.json` entry names the robot's description, its MJCF, its mount body, the MJCF body of each URDF link the MJCF names otherwise and the gains of its arms; that release pinned in Waldo's `web/public/assets/catalogue.lock.json` and in `sim_base_images/waldo_catalogue.py` (`RELEASE_DIGEST`); and `sim_mujoco/engine/models/<id>.json5`. [sim_mujoco's README](https://github.com/Peppy-bot/nodes-hub/tree/main/sim_mujoco#models) says what each holds.
3. Give the Isaac Sim node its model: a USD bundle of the robot, pinned by key and SHA-256 in `sim_base_images/isaac_assets.env` and staged by `sim_base_images/download_assets.sh`, and `sim_isaac/engine/models/<id>.json5`. [sim_isaac's README](https://github.com/Peppy-bot/nodes-hub/tree/main/sim_isaac#adding-a-model) says what each holds.
4. Move the pins of this package, of `robot_description_py` and of `control_core_py` in `sim_base_images/requirements.mujoco.txt`, `sim_base_images/requirements.isaac.txt` and each node's `tests/pyproject.toml` to the commit that carries the description and step 1, bump `MUJOCO_IMAGE_REV` and `ISAAC_IMAGE_REV` in `sim_base_images/build_base_images.sh`, and run that script, so each base image bakes the new pins.
